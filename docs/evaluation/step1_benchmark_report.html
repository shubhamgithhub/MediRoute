What we found

safetyMethod 4 almost never answers wrongly

Confidently wrong 1–7%, against 26–34% for the single methods on lay-language messages (gretel).

costBut it asks too often

Clarification on 30% (core50), 32% (mediroute) and 58% (gretel) of messages, so accuracy before the patient answers drops to 34–68%.

rankingIts options are good

Recall@3 90–99% and the best NDCG@3 on mediroute: when it asks, the right speciality is usually among the choices offered.

tie-breakNeither voter can simply win ties

On disagreements the lookup is right more often on team-written cases (15 vs 3), the LLM on lay language (60 vs 25).

LLMThe small LLM alone is not enough

LLM only: 73–81% accuracy with 16–26% confidently wrong. Ungrounded AI is the weakest real method on our own sets.

AI valueLLM evidence improves search

Searching the LLM-extracted phrases beats searching the raw text on mediroute (93% vs 87–88%), a measured gain from the AI step.

How the benchmark works

Every method gets exactly the same patient text and is scored against an answer written in advance. Method 4 is replayed on the LLM outputs saved from the same run, so it differs from the current pipeline only in design, not in LLM randomness.

Read these numbers with care

  • Gold labels are drafts. core50 and mediroute were written by the team; doctor review is pending. The TA asked for independently reviewed cases.
  • Team-written cases are easy for search. They use textbook terms that match ICD descriptions, so semantic search scores 92% on core50 but 73% on lay language.
  • gretel favours General Medicine. It is an accepted answer for 153 of 212 cases, so always answering General Medicine scores 72%. Pipelines that ask before settling on General Medicine are penalised there.
  • Pipelines are scored before the patient answers. A question counts as not correct. A simulated clarification answer would give a fairer final score; not built yet.
  • Small local LLM. llama3.2 3B. A larger model may change the LLM-only and method 4 results; one run only, so LLM variance is not measured.

How much accuracy can a tie-break win?

Computed from the saved votes of this run, no new LLM calls. "Perfect judge" always picks the voter that was right and asks when both were wrong: it is the ceiling for any tie-break, not a prediction.

A real judge lands between the simple rules and the ceiling. The gain is largest on lay language (gretel): up to 84% accuracy with 7% confidently wrong, against 54% and 34% today. The cases where both voters are wrong (2, 4 and 16 cases) no tie-break can fix; that needs better retrieval (CCSR, scoring) or a better LLM.

Option 5 in detail: an LLM judge that cannot invent answers

The judge runs only when the hybrid lookup and the LLM voter disagree. It only chooses; it never writes text for the patient. Any failed check means the system asks the patient instead.

Judge metrics to add to the benchmark: invalid-output rate, citation-failure rate, order-swap flip rate, accuracy on disagreements against T2 and T3, and confidently wrong after the judge (must stay at or below method 4's 1–7%). The explanation shown to the patient is built from verified evidence, not from LLM wording, and the judge never overrides the emergency rules.
