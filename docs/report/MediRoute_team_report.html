In one page

  • What it does: a patient describes the problem in their own words; MediRoute suggests which speciality to search (Step 1) and lists verified hospitals for it in the chosen area, with the reasons (Step 2).
  • What it does not do: no diagnosis, no treatment advice, no booking. Procedures are used only when the patient wrote them.
  • How AI is used: an LLM extracts the evidence and votes; a guarded LLM judge settles disagreements. Code checks every AI output (closed lists, quotes must be in the text) and asks the patient when unsure.
  • Measured: every design change was benchmarked against baselines (keyword search, meaning search, LLM only, voting). Where AI did not beat simple rules (Step 2 capability matching), we did not use it.
  • Open: test labels and mapping rules await doctor review; hospital procedure lists are unverified; 201 hospitals only. Work after 11 Nov is local and not yet committed.

Who and why

  • Patients (including international patients) looking for treatment in India who do not know which department to approach.
  • Problem: symptoms and lay words do not map obviously to a speciality, and hospital information online is unverified and often commercial.
  • MediRoute narrows the search: one speciality to search, then hospitals whose speciality is confirmed on their own website, NABH / JCI accreditation shown.

Design rules

  • Discovery, not advice: “Suggested speciality to search”, never a diagnosis; a disclaimer on every result.
  • Ask when unsure: a short question beats a confident wrong answer; the patient's answer is used in the decision.
  • Explain every result: the evidence words for the speciality and the ranking reasons for each hospital.
  • Emergency words (chest pain with breathlessness, suicidal intent…) show an emergency notice first; routing continues.
  • Fix data at the root: no one-off override rows for single cases.

Why a portal and not a chatbot

  • Answers come only from a fixed, checked hospital list (no invented hospitals or departments).
  • Every request is logged step by step (admin page), so any answer can be audited.
  • Every stage is measured on test sets; section 9 compares a general AI on the same questions.

Key choices

  • Two separate steps: Step 1 decides the speciality; Step 2 only ranks hospitals for it. Each is tested on its own.

9 · Compared with a general AI

  • General AI: google/gemini-4-carbon via OpenRouter (low reasoning effort: a direct chat answer), given the patient's own words plus “Which medical speciality should I see, and which hospitals would you recommend? Please keep the answer short.” No system prompt, no hospital list, no tools.
  • Its free-text answers were turned into data by openai/gpt-6-luna (speciality mapped to our 26; hospitals named), then scored with the same test sets and website grades as MediRoute. Test cost $0.49.

What it shows

  • Speciality: level. The general AI scores slightly higher on correct answers (89% vs 85%) and asks less (4% vs 12%); it is confidently wrong more often (7% vs 3%), but 6 of its 7 misses send the patient to emergency care for chest-pain or breathing symptoms (cautious, not careless). None of these gaps is statistically significant (Fisher p = 0.53 and 0.33). We do not claim better speciality accuracy than a strong general AI.
  • Fair input: both systems got the same text: the message plus the report text for the 15 core50 report cases (a first run without the reports made the AI ask for them and was discarded).
  • Hospitals: the clear difference is verifiability. 38% of the hospitals it names cannot be checked against our NABH / JCI / website-verified list (often a local branch of a chain, or a hospital we never collected). It did keep to the patient's city. Every MediRoute result is from the verified list, with its reasons.
  • Quality of the hospitals it names trends lower (confirmed capable 76% vs 86%; Capable@3 78% vs 93%), but the samples are small (p = 0.12 and 0.07): a direction, not proof.
  • Read with care: a hospital not in our 201 is “not verifiable”, not necessarily wrong. NDCG favours MediRoute partly because its list could draw on 20 graded candidates while the general AI named at most 5, so the ‘confirmed capable’ share is the fairer quality measure. The API model is not the ChatGPT or Google app, which may also search the web. Hospital names were matched by code (chains: the AI's city, else the patient's area).

12 · Limitations and next steps

Limitations

  • Labels and rules are drafts: test labels, 1,381 mapping rules and body-area rules await the doctor review; four “wrong” portal cases have questionable labels.
  • Hospital data: 201 hospitals; procedure lists come from the team sheet and are unverified (~50% recall), which limits procedure matching.
  • Grades: Step 2 grades are model-drafted from website snippets; 20% human spot-check pending; sites that block automated reading cannot be graded.
  • Scope: no costs, doctor profiles, availability or booking; English only.
  • State of the code: the 12 Nov work (fixes, screens, themes, comparison) is local and not yet committed.

Next steps

  • Doctor review of the drafts (docs/review/MediRoute_doctor_review.pdf) and the 20% grade spot-check.
  • Offline website extraction to fill the procedure lists, measured with the same Step 2 harness.
  • Commit the 12 Nov work and update the README (setup section still lists the old free models).
  • Pick the final colour theme and remove the selector; replace the home-page demo card with real results.
